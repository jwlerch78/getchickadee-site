Setting Up Chickadee Voice on Home Assistant

Last updated: June 2026

Chickadee Voice brings premium voice and AI to your Chickadee tablets and Home Assistant voice satellites — with the brain running in Chickadee's cloud or on your own local model. This guide walks you through installing the two Home Assistant pieces you need, signing in, and configuring everything from the Chickadee Console.

Beta program

Chickadee Voice is currently in a limited beta. Your Chickadee account must be enabled for the beta before the Voice & AI controls appear in the Console. Request beta access here.

What you'll need

  • A Home Assistant instance (HAOS or supervised) you can install add-ons on
  • HACS installed (for the Chickadee integration)
  • A Chickadee account enabled for the beta
  • One or more Chickadee tablets, and/or an HA voice satellite
  • (Optional) A local LLM endpoint if you want to run the AI on your own hardware

The pieces, and what each does

Chickadee Voice on Home Assistant uses two components that work together:

  • The Chickadee Console add-on — runs inside Home Assistant, signs in to your Chickadee account, and is your control panel. It's the login and credential hub for the whole household.
  • The Chickadee integration — the runtime gateway. It lets your tablets and satellites reach Chickadee Voice over your existing Home Assistant URL (on your LAN or remotely), and provides the voice entities for HA satellites.

Part 1: Install the Chickadee Console add-on

1

Open the Add-on Store

In Home Assistant, go to Settings → Add-ons → Add-on Store.

2

Add the Chickadee repository (if needed)

If "Chickadee Console" isn't listed, click the three-dot menu in the top right → Repositories, paste the Chickadee add-on repository URL, and click Add. The add-on will then appear in the store.

3

Install and start

Open Chickadee Console, click Install, then Start. Turn on Start on boot and Show in sidebar so it's always available.

4

Open the Console and sign in

Click Open Web UI (or the Chickadee Console entry in the HA sidebar) and sign in with your Chickadee account. This account holds your household's credits and is what the whole house shares.

Add-on configuration

In the add-on's Configuration tab you can set the environment (leave it on the default unless we tell you otherwise) and the log level. Most people never need to change these.

Part 2: Install the Chickadee integration

The integration is what lets your tablets and satellites actually use Chickadee Voice. The easiest way to install it is through HACS.

1

Open the install link from the Console

In the Chickadee Console, look for the Install the Chickadee integration button (a HACS one-click link). Click it to open the Chickadee repository directly in HACS, then click Download.

2

Or add it in HACS manually

Alternatively, open HACS, search for Chickadee, and click Download.

3

Restart Home Assistant

After downloading, restart Home Assistant so the integration loads. It will set itself up automatically; if HA prompts you to configure a discovered Chickadee device, confirm it.

Why both pieces?

The add-on holds your login and credits; the integration is reachable over your Home Assistant URL, so it works on your LAN and through remote access (Nabu Casa / reverse proxy) with no extra setup. You need both for Chickadee Voice to reach your devices.

Part 3: Choose your Assistant

Everything from here happens in the Console. Open Chickadee Console → Voice & AI. The top-level choice is Assistant, with two options:

  • Home Assistant — routes voice through a Home Assistant Assist pipeline you select. HA handles speech, the agent, and the response. Free, no Chickadee credits used.
  • Chickadee Voice — Chickadee orchestrates the whole thing. This is what unlocks premium speech recognition, AI models (cloud or local), built-in tools, and personality voices.

Select Chickadee Voice. You'll see a one-time privacy summary — read it, then continue. Choosing Chickadee Voice reveals three groups: AI Model, Voice, and Tools.

Local vs. cloud, color-coded

As you configure each component, Chickadee marks it Local or Cloud. Anything cloud is called out at the moment you pick it, so "local model" never quietly pairs with cloud speech. To go fully private, choose a local model and local speech and local voice.

AI Model

Pick which brain answers open-ended questions:

  • Claude or Gemini — Chickadee's cloud models, tier-labeled so you can see which are economical and which are premium. Cloud
  • My Local LLM — point Chickadee at your own model. You'll enter an endpoint URL and pick a model from the supported (“blessed”) list, or choose “custom.” Chickadee validates the endpoint and warns you if the model isn't on the supported list. Local

Local-model hardware matters

A reliable local model wants a capable, always-on host — roughly a 20B-class model on 16 GB+ of VRAM, kept warm. Smaller boxes (a Raspberry Pi, HA Green/Yellow) can't run a usable model for voice. If you're unsure, start with a cloud model and switch to local once your hardware is set up.

Voice

Choose how Chickadee speaks back. This is independent of the model, so "local model + premium personality voice" is a perfectly valid combo.

  • Local / native voice — free, on-device or HA-side text-to-speech. Local
  • Chickadee personality voice — premium character voices your family picks. Draws on credits. Cloud

Tools

Turn individual knowledge tools on or off: Web search, Sports, Weather, Places, and more. These let Chickadee answer real-world questions with live, accurate data instead of guessing.

HA Voice Agent

When Home Assistant is connected, set the HA Voice Agent — the Home Assistant conversation agent that Chickadee routes device commands to. Leave it on the default conversation.home_assistant (Home Assistant's fast, free, built-in agent) unless you have a specific reason to change it. Chickadee handles general and world-knowledge questions itself; device control goes here.

AI defaults reference

These live under Voice & AI and apply to every device on your account:

SettingWhat it does
Web searchLets the AI search the web for answers it doesn't already have.
Retrieve picturesLets the AI reference your family photos in responses.
Conversation memoryRemembers the last few turns so follow-ups make sense. Set how long it's kept.
Keep transcriptsSaves voice transcripts and replies so you can review them in Usage.

Part 4: Set up personalities

Personalities shape how Chickadee talks — tone, character, and which voice it uses. They're shared across your whole household. In the Console, open the Personalities section under Voice & AI.

1

Start from a built-in template

Browse the built-in personalities (Chickadee is the default). You can add a Family notes override to any template — private context about your household that helps responses feel personal — without editing the template itself.

2

Or create a custom personality

Click New Personality and fill in the name, overview, adjectives, topics, and a few example phrases. Add family notes for household context.

3

Pick a voice

Choose the voice for this personality. Preferred lets a device override it; Fixed locks the personality to that voice everywhere.

Part 5: Turn on household sharing (optional)

Household sharing lets anonymous Chickadee tablets (no Chickadee login) and HA voice satellites on your network use Chickadee Voice through your account — one login powering a whole house of devices.

1

Open Account & Credits

In the Console, go to Account & Credits. This section appears when you're signed in through the add-on.

2

Enable Household Chickadee Cloud Sharing

Toggle Household sharing on. It's off by default. Once on, kiosk tablets and satellites in your home can use Chickadee Voice without their own login.

Sharing uses your credits

Every device that uses Chickadee Voice through household sharing draws on your account's credit balance. That's the point — one account funds the household — but it's why the toggle is off by default and shows a disclaimer.

Part 6: Test it

You can test without saying a word out loud. In the Console, open the AI Chat tab under Voice & AI. Pick a personality and model, type a question, and send it. You'll see the response along with the model used, token usage, and latency — a quick way to confirm everything is wired up.

Then try it by voice on a device:

  • "Chickadee, what's the weather this weekend?" — should answer with live weather.
  • "Chickadee, turn on the living room lights." — should run locally through Home Assistant.
  • "Chickadee, what was the score of the game?" — should pull live sports data.

One config, every device

Everything you set here is account-level. Add a second tablet or satellite and it adopts the same Assistant, model, voice, tools, and personality automatically — no per-device setup.

Troubleshooting

The Voice & AI page is missing in the Console

  • Your account may not be enabled for the beta yet — request access or check back after we confirm.
  • Make sure you're signed in to the right Chickadee account in the add-on.

A kiosk tablet can't use Chickadee Voice

  • Confirm the add-on is signed in and household sharing is on.
  • Confirm the Chickadee integration is installed and Home Assistant has been restarted.
  • Check that the tablet can reach your Home Assistant URL.

Device commands go to the AI instead of running locally

  • Set the HA Voice Agent to conversation.home_assistant so device control uses HA's fast local agent.

Responses are slow with a local model

  • Your host may be too small, or the model is cold-loading. Use a capable, always-on host and keep the model warm, or switch to a cloud model.
