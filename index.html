A Home Assistant dashboard and voice assistant for a wall tablet.

Put a cheap Android tablet on the wall and Chickadee turns it into your Home Assistant dashboard — with a wake word, so you can just talk to it. It runs on your hardware, it does not need an account, and it keeps working when your internet doesn't.

Free No account Works offline AGPL-3.0

What it is

Two pieces that work independently, so you can start with either.

The add-on

A Home Assistant add-on that gives every Assist device you already own — Voice PE, ESPHome satellites, your phone — an assistant with a real brain behind it. No screen required. For a lot of people this is the whole product.

The tablet app

An Android app for a wall tablet, an old Fire TV, or anything else that takes an APK. Your Home Assistant dashboards, camera feeds, and music — plus hands-free voice with an on-device wake word.

How the voice works

Chickadee has no hosted service of its own, so every stage — speech-to-text, the language model, and the voice that answers — runs on something you control. Mix them however you like; you can change any stage without touching the others.

Home Assistant's own engines

Whatever you already have wired into Assist — Whisper, Piper, a local Ollama conversation agent. Chickadee uses them as-is.

Your own API key

Paste a key for Google, OpenAI, or OpenRouter and get the quality of a frontier model. Calls go from your box to that provider, billed to your account. The key is stored on your box and nowhere else.

A model on your own network

Point it at your own LLM and speech servers and nothing leaves your network at all. See below for what this actually costs in hardware.

Installing it

  1. Add the Chickadee repository in Home Assistant under Settings → Add-ons → Add-on Store → ⋮ → Repositories, then install and start the add-on.
  2. Open the Chickadee panel in your sidebar and restart Home Assistant when it asks. Every Assist device in the house now has a Chickadee voice assistant available.
  3. Optionally, sideload the Android app onto a tablet and point it at your Home Assistant.

What it doesn't do

The things you would otherwise find out three evenings in. If any of these is a dealbreaker, better to know now.

  • There is no hosted option. Chickadee has no account and no service to sign in to, which means there is no "just works" path that skips setup. You bring an API key or you run your own models. That is deliberate — but it is still setup.
  • A fully local model needs real hardware. A GPU or an Apple-silicon box on your network answers in a couple of seconds. A small CPU-only Home Assistant box takes minutes per reply, which is not a usable assistant. This is the most common disappointment, so plan the hardware before you plan the privacy.
  • No web search or image search out of the box. Those run through paid APIs. If your model does its own grounded search (Gemini does) that works, and a self-hosted SearXNG works — but nothing built in searches the web for free.
  • No calendar, chores, or family location features. Those belong to a different product; the About page says which. Chickadee is Home Assistant, cameras, music, voice.
  • Pull requests are not accepted. Bug reports and feature requests genuinely are. This is a read-and-fork project with one maintainer, and saying so is more honest than leaving open a contribution path that dead-ends.
  • It is heavily AI-assisted, human-reviewed, by one maintainer, on top of a voice stack that has been running in real households since 2025. That is why the commit history is short and fast. Judge the code.
