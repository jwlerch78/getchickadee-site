What it doesn't do

The things you would otherwise find out three evenings in. If any of these is a dealbreaker, better to know now.

  • Voice needs your own AI key, or your own model. There is no hosted option — no account, nothing to sign in to — so the brain has to run somewhere you provide. Everything else is already on your box: speech and voices use Home Assistant's own Whisper and Piper, weather comes from your HA entities, scores and images need no key at all. Web search wants one more free key if you want it. The full list, and how many keys that really is.
  • A fully local model needs real hardware. A GPU or an Apple-silicon box on your network answers in a couple of seconds. A small CPU-only Home Assistant box takes minutes per reply, which is not a usable assistant. This is the most common disappointment, so plan the hardware before you plan the privacy.
  • Every account is yours to create. No single sign-up covers it — the AI key is one account, and web search is another if you want it. That is more setup than a product with a company behind it, and it is the trade: your keys, your quotas, your bills, nothing metered through us.
  • No calendar, chores, or family location features. Those belong to a different product; the About page says which. Chickadee is Home Assistant, cameras, music, and voice.
  • Pull requests are not accepted. Bug reports and feature requests genuinely are. This is a read-and-fork project with one maintainer, and saying so is more honest than leaving open a contribution path that dead-ends.
  • It is heavily AI-assisted, human-reviewed, by one maintainer, on top of a voice stack that has been running in real households since 2025. That is why the commit history is short and fast. Judge the code.
