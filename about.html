About this project

Chickadee is a Home Assistant dashboard and voice assistant for a wall tablet. It is free, needs no account, and runs on hardware you own. This page answers the questions you would otherwise have to reverse-engineer from the commit log.

Who builds it

One maintainer. Chickadee is built by the maker of Dashie, a paid family dashboard product, and the two share a codebase — the voice stack underneath Chickadee is the one that has been running in real households since 2025. They are separate products with separate audiences: Dashie is a subscription product aimed at families who want calendar, chores and location features, and Chickadee is the Home Assistant one, free and account-free.

That relationship is stated here because you are entitled to know it, not because there is anything you need to do about it. Chickadee has no upsell, no trial, no sign-in prompt, and no path that leads to a payment page; it works forever without any of that. If you want the longer version — which parts of the cloud are not published and why, how the money works, and what this repository's history looks like — the PROVENANCE.md file in the repository is written to be checked rather than skimmed.

What's published, and what isn't

"Open source" gets used loosely, so here is the specific version, accurate as of August 1, 2026.

  • The add-on — server, console, and AI brain runtime. AGPL-3.0, published, and genuinely self-hostable: with your own engines, nothing contacts a service of ours, because there isn't one.
  • The Home Assistant integrations — AGPL-3.0, published: voice & Assist and device.
  • The Android app — the source mirror is planned and is not published yet. Until it is, the app is a binary you download, not something you can build. That distinction is worth more than the word "open," so it is stated plainly rather than left to be discovered.

How it's built

Chickadee is heavily AI-assisted and human-reviewed, by one person, on top of a mature voice stack. That is why the commit history is short and fast, and it is said here rather than only in the repository because you should not have to go looking for it. Judge the code.

Pull requests are not accepted — this is a read-and-fork project, not a collaborative one. Bug reports and feature requests are genuinely wanted; real-world Home Assistant setups are the one thing a single maintainer cannot manufacture.

The name

A chickadee is a small bird with a very recognisable call, which seemed right for something that sits on a wall waiting to be spoken to. One of the shipped wake-word models is also called chickadee; nothing selects it for you, and which wake word you pick changes nothing about where your audio goes.
