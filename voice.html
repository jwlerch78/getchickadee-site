✦ Chickadee Voice

Voice AI for Home Assistant, tuned for a household.

The models, tools, prompts, and voices already wired together — running on whichever brain you point it at.

Good household voice for HA usually means evenings of assembling models, tools, voices, and prompts, then tuning them until answers stop being useless. Chickadee ships that assembly done: control your smart home, ask real questions, and hear the answer in a voice your household picked. Bring your AI key — optionally one more for web search — and everything else runs on the box you already have.

Chickadee answering a spoken question over a Home Assistant dashboard, with a result card on screen

Bring your own keys — here is exactly how many

Chickadee has no hosted service, so nothing runs on our account. You bring the brain. Almost everything else already runs on the Home Assistant box you have. Stated up front rather than discovered in a setup screen:

WhatRuns onKey needed
The AI brain Your key for Google, OpenAI or OpenRouter — or your own model on your own network Yes — this is the one
Web search Tavily Optional — free tier, 1,000 searches a month, no card
Speech to text Home Assistant's own Whisper None
Text to speech Home Assistant's own Piper None
Weather Your Home Assistant weather entities None
Sports scores ESPN None
Images Wikimedia None

So the realistic ask is one key, or two if you want the assistant to answer questions about the world. Not the six-provider scavenger hunt that "bring your own everything" usually means.

It is still more setup than a product with an account behind it, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. That friction is the point. Every one of those accounts is yours — your key, your quota, your bill, your relationship with the provider. Nothing is metered through us, because there is no us in the path. If Chickadee disappeared tomorrow the keys would keep working for whatever else you pointed them at, and the box would keep running.

Optional upgrades, if you want them

Home Assistant's own engines are the default and cost nothing. These are swaps, not requirements — each is your own account, and the assistant works fully without any of them.

Sharper speech recognition

Whisper on your box is solid. A hosted speech provider handles household names, unusual words and cross-room audio noticeably better. Deepgram gives $200 of credit on signup with no card.

More natural voices

Piper is clear and completely local. Hosted voices sound better — ElevenLabs' free tier does include API access, but at 10,000 characters a month it is a taste rather than a daily driver. Inworld's free tier is roughly 40 minutes a month.

Wider image results

Wikimedia needs no key and covers most of what gets asked for. Pexels is a free key if you want a broader library, and it asks for a credit link alongside each photo.

Why bother, over raw Assist

Three things that separate this from stock Home Assistant, Alexa, or Google.

Assembled, not just available

Tools, prompts, voices, and the dialog loop come wired together and tuned for spoken answers — the part that normally eats your evenings. Every piece is swappable if you disagree with a choice.

Adaptive context

It sends the model only what your question needs — the relevant devices and their states, not your whole house. Faster, cheaper on your own key, and more accurate.

Your model, your account

Gemini, OpenAI, OpenRouter, or Ollama on your own box — you pick, and you hold the key. No proxy of ours in the middle, nothing metered, no lock-in and nothing to cancel.

You decide what leaves your home

Every stage of the pipeline is a separate choice, and the console colour-codes each one so you can see at a glance whether it stays on your network.

Stays on your network

  • Your own LLM — home and device data never leaves the LAN
  • On-device speech recognition and local text-to-speech, for a fully offline stack
  • Device control always runs locally, and works with no internet at all
  • No account and no ongoing cost — you are paying for your own hardware and nothing else

Goes to a provider you chose

  • A hosted model can be far cheaper than hardware capable of running a good one locally
  • Stronger speech recognition and more natural voices than a small local box can manage
  • Audio and text go directly from your box to that provider, on your key, under your agreement with them
  • No Chickadee server sits in the middle — there isn't one. We never see it and cannot meter it
Pick a local model alongside local speech and local voices and nothing leaves your LAN at any point — no lookups, no telemetry, no version check. That configuration is fully supported, not a degraded mode.

What you get

All of it on the same wake word.

Set it up once, for every device

Configure in the console and every tablet and satellite in the house adopts it. No per-device fuss.

Optimised prompting

The model is called only when it is needed, with the context it needs, and answers are shaped for being heard rather than read.

Instant for the common case

Device and music commands resolve locally and sub-second, without touching a model at all. Only open-ended questions go to the brain — which on your own key means most of what you say costs nothing.

Local-first resilience

Device control runs locally, so lights and scenes keep working when your internet is down — or when the provider is.

Wake word on the device

Detection runs on the tablet or your satellite, not in the cloud. Custom-trained models ship with it, and the community ones work too.

Voices & personalities

Pick the character your household wants to hear. Runs on Home Assistant's own Piper by default — local, offline, no key. Swap in a hosted voice on your own key if you want it to sound better.

Speech recognition

Home Assistant's own Whisper transcribes on your box by default — offline and keyless. A hosted provider on your own key is sharper on household names and across a room, if you want the upgrade.

Web answers

Ask about something outside the house and it looks it up. Tavily's free tier covers 1,000 searches a month with no card, a model with its own grounded search covers it too, and a self-hosted SearXNG keeps even this on your network.

Weather and scores, no key

Weather comes from the Home Assistant weather entities you already have. Sports scores come from ESPN, which needs no key and no account — ask for last night's result and it just answers.
