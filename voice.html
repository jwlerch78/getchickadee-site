✦ Chickadee Voice

Voice AI for Home Assistant, tuned for a household.

The models, tools, prompts, and voices already wired together — running on whichever brain you point it at.

Good household voice for HA usually means evenings of assembling models, tools, voices, and prompts, then tuning them until answers stop being useless. Chickadee ships that assembly done: control your smart home, ask real questions, and hear the answer in a voice your household picked. You supply the brain — your own API key, or a model on your own network.

Chickadee answering a spoken question over a Home Assistant dashboard, with a result card on screen

What setup actually looks like

Stated here rather than discovered later. Chickadee has no hosted service, so before voice answers anything you point it at a brain. There are three ways, and none of them involves an account with us:

  • Home Assistant's own engines. If you already run Whisper, Piper, or a local conversation agent in Assist, Chickadee uses them as they are. Nothing new to sign up for.
  • Your own API key. Paste a key from Google, OpenAI, or OpenRouter into the console. About a minute, and it buys you frontier-model comprehension. You pay that provider directly, at their prices; we never see the traffic and take no cut.
  • A model on your own network. Point it at your own LLM and speech servers and nothing leaves your LAN at all.

We would rather lead with this than have you find it in the setup screen after being told it works out of the box. It doesn't work out of the box — it works about a minute after you choose a brain, and then it keeps working with no bill from us, ever.

Why bother, over raw Assist

Three things that separate this from stock Home Assistant, Alexa, or Google.

Assembled, not just available

Tools, prompts, voices, and the dialog loop come wired together and tuned for spoken answers — the part that normally eats your evenings. Every piece is swappable if you disagree with a choice.

Adaptive context

It sends the model only what your question needs — the relevant devices and their states, not your whole house. Faster, cheaper on your own key, and more accurate.

Your model, your account

Gemini, OpenAI, OpenRouter, or Ollama on your own box — you pick, and you hold the key. No proxy of ours in the middle, nothing metered, no lock-in and nothing to cancel.

You decide what leaves your home

Every stage of the pipeline is a separate choice, and the console colour-codes each one so you can see at a glance whether it stays on your network.

Stays on your network

  • Your own LLM — home and device data never leaves the LAN
  • On-device speech recognition and local text-to-speech, for a fully offline stack
  • Device control always runs locally, and works with no internet at all
  • No account and no ongoing cost — you are paying for your own hardware and nothing else

Goes to a provider you chose

  • A hosted model can be far cheaper than hardware capable of running a good one locally
  • Stronger speech recognition and more natural voices than a small local box can manage
  • Audio and text go directly from your box to that provider, on your key, under your agreement with them
  • No Chickadee server sits in the middle — there isn't one. We never see it and cannot meter it
Pick a local model alongside local speech and local voices and nothing leaves your LAN at any point — no lookups, no telemetry, no version check. That configuration is fully supported, not a degraded mode.

What you get

All of it on the same wake word.

Set it up once, for every device

Configure in the console and every tablet and satellite in the house adopts it. No per-device fuss.

Optimised prompting

The model is called only when it is needed, with the context it needs, and answers are shaped for being heard rather than read.

Instant for the common case

Device and music commands resolve locally and sub-second, without touching a model at all. Only open-ended questions go to the brain — which on your own key means most of what you say costs nothing.

Local-first resilience

Device control runs locally, so lights and scenes keep working when your internet is down — or when the provider is.

Wake word on the device

Detection runs on the tablet or your satellite, not in the cloud. Custom-trained models ship with it, and the community ones work too.

Voices & personalities

Pick the character your household wants to hear. Local engines like Piper work; a hosted voice provider on your own key sounds better.

Better speech recognition

On-device transcription is included and works offline. A hosted speech provider on your key handles household names and unusual words considerably better.

Web answers

Nothing built in searches the web for free. A model with its own grounded search (Gemini) covers it, or point it at a self-hosted SearXNG.

Follow-up questions without re-saying the wake word, and background chatter ignored instead of looping.
